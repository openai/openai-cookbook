PHENOMENA · 001 · English

The Committee for the Quality of Reality

Ilya Doroshenko

Marcus Aurelius once said something along these lines: never let anyone hear you complain about life. Not even you.

Which is excellent advice, especially coming from a Roman emperor.

His average Monday probably began with a rebellion, a plague, several Germanic tribes crossing a border, no money in the treasury, and some cousin announcing that he had recently given the matter serious thought and would make a much better Caesar.

So the man had material.

Keeping calm in front of other people is usually the easy part. You nod. You say, “Right, understood.” You leave the room.

Then you stand in the corridor for ten minutes, staring into the middle distance like a man who has just received an invoice from the universe for services he never ordered.

The real show starts inside.

That is where the Committee for the Quality of Reality gathers for another emergency meeting.

The chairman clears his throat. The secretary opens the file. The experts present their findings.

People could think before acting.

Money could arrive when promised.

Documents could be processed correctly the first time.

A government website could, just once, open the page you actually need without demanding a digital certificate, three passwords, a Java update, and the baptism record of the system administrator.

The committee is absolutely right.

Reality, meanwhile, has already left the building.

It delayed the payment, sent the wrong person, lost the document, moved the meeting, forgot to call back, and went somewhere for lunch.

And we are still negotiating with it.

“Dear Ilya,

Thank you for bringing this matter to our attention.

Following a full internal investigation, Monday has been declared defective. The responsible idiot has been replaced. The funds have been credited. Everyone involved has attended a short course in basic common sense and would like to offer their sincere apologies.”

The universe has a peculiar customer support department.

It accepts every complaint.

It mostly replies with weather.

And here is the funny part.

The actual situation usually requires a fairly small number of actions.

Make the call. Change the agreement. Fix the document. Wait. Leave. Pay. Take your things. Return somebody else’s things. Occasionally say, “Fuck this,” and go have lunch, which is also a perfectly respectable management decision.

But beside the real task, we create another one.

We begin proving, internally and at great length, that this situation should never have existed.

So one hand solves the problem, while the other writes a formal complaint to the Department of Cosmic Administration.

Then we wonder why we are tired.

It is like travelling with a heavy suitcase.

The suitcase is heavy. This information has already been established.

Still, you may spend the whole journey explaining to it how heavy it is, how badly it is behaving, and how modern, properly educated suitcases generally have wheels and a more constructive attitude.

The suitcase will listen with the calm dignity of a suitcase.

Its weight will remain exactly the same.

At that point, you have options.

Throw things away. Fix the handle. Buy wheels. Ask for help. Leave the suitcase at the hotel. Or open it and discover that half of it contains objects packed for events that have never happened to anyone in recorded history.

All of these choices create movement.

Inner indignation creates a lifetime subscription to an old situation.

Buddhists call this the second arrow.

The first arrow is what happens.

The second is the extra one we fire into ourselves while insisting that what happened had no right to happen.

And the second arrow is often far more gifted than the first.

It follows you into the shower. It joins you for breakfast. It turns up in the car. It gives its finest performances at three in the morning, long after every other participant in the original situation has gone home, fallen asleep, or forgotten the whole thing.

The event may have ended months ago.

Its farewell tour continues indefinitely.

Acceptance has nothing to do with surrender.

It certainly has nothing to do with smiling beautifully and pretending that everything is wonderful.

Acceptance is simply the end of negotiations with a fact.

This happened.

This is what remains.

This is where the next step goes.

Yesterday will not come back, change clothes, apologise, and perform its role with greater professionalism.

You can remain completely right.

You can know exactly who fucked everything up.

You can keep the documents, the messages, the screenshots, and a beautifully organised folder called FINAL_EVIDENCE_v7_REAL_FINAL.

But giving somebody else’s stupidity your morning, your sleep, your appetite, your attention, and several more years of your life is a terrible deal.

Strength returns quietly.

No trumpet. No revelation. No heroic music.

The meeting simply ends.

The committee goes home.

The secretary closes the file.

The world remains whatever strange thing it has managed to become.

And your life becomes yours again.
